70 of 599 pages · machine reading, not proofread · 20-Nov-2026

The secular part of the history of Gregory Barhebraeus (1226–1286), in Paul Bedjan's vocalised edition, Gregorii Barhebraei Chronicon Syriacum (Paris 1890), from the University of Toronto scan. The Syriac was read by Kraken with Beth Mardutho's Qoruyo models for printed East Syriac (CC BY 4.0). On each page the Syriac is on the right, and on the left are the same words in Hebrew letters, by a fixed letter table (one letter for one consonant; vowels left out), for readers of Hebrew and Aramaic.

How good is it? On p. 128 (highlighted: the plague of locusts of the year 1095 of the Greeks, AD 784) the reading was compared with a text of the same passage made from two other prints in another script: 55 of 58 words were the same. The vowel points, the page headers and the notes have not been checked. Method and code: github.com/EzraBrand/barhebraeus-chronography.
